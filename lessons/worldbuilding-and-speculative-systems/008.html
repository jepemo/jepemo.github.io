Worldbuilding and Speculative Systems

008 25 min beginner CAPSTONE

Capstone: Build a Speculative World Brief

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to...

  • Produce a compact speculative world brief that connects constraints, daily life, institutions, capabilities, culture, geography, and history.

  • Test whether another creator can make grounded choices from the brief without asking for an encyclopedia.

  • Review the brief for causal coherence, narrative pressure, selective detail, and productive openness.

Idea in one sentence: A strong world brief gives another creator enough connected structure to make consequences and enough open space to discover something you did not prewrite.

Core Insight

Imagine that you hand Nima to another creator.

You will not be present to explain the water system, the Cistern Authority, sunstones, empty cups, Mara's warning, or the disputed ledger. The creator wants to design a scenario about the council vote. They have one page and one question:

What can someone in this world choose, and why will the world push back?

An encyclopedia is not the answer. A list of disconnected facts is not the answer either.

The creator needs a speculative world brief: a compact artifact that connects the world's pressures well enough for someone else to act inside it.

The brief must do two things at once:

  • establish rules and consequences that support fair decisions
  • leave questions that invite investigation, interpretation, and invention

Too little structure makes every outcome arbitrary. Too much explanation turns the brief into a closed museum display.

The capstone is to build the useful middle.

The Scenario

Use Nima as the worked example.

At sunset, the council will decide whether marsh workers may warn Lower Gate directly when they detect contaminated water. The Cistern Authority wants all warnings to pass through its Hall. Mara holds a checkpoint tablet that suggests her warning during the Year of Bitter Water reached the city before distribution began.

Today, a cracked sunstone has failed in Lower Gate.

This situation needs every major layer from the track:

  • a material constraint: scarce, unsafe water
  • ordinary routines: settling, storing, charging, testing, and rationing
  • an institution: the Authority controls sluices and official warnings
  • a bounded capability: sunstones sterilize one jar under specific conditions
  • culture and memory: empty cups and black reed carry disputed meanings
  • geography and time: warnings, water, orders, and records move at different speeds
  • narrative pressure: Mara must decide whether to reveal evidence before the vote

The brief will connect these layers around that choice.

Design Goal

Write a brief that another creator can use to do all of the following:

  1. Predict at least three consequences of a changed rule.
  2. Identify who gains options and who loses control.
  3. Place one action on a plausible route and timeline.
  4. Interpret one symbol differently from two social positions.
  5. Find two open questions worth investigating.

If the creator can only repeat your facts, the brief is not ready.

If the creator can make any outcome happen without violating anything, the brief is also not ready.

The Brief Structure

Use nine compact sections. Each section should connect to at least one other section.

1. World promise

Write one sentence that names the central pressure and the kind of choices the world supports.

In Nima, survival depends on moving water, warnings, and authority through unequal routes, while the city publicly argues about who saved it from an earlier disaster.

2. Non-negotiable constraints

Choose two to four rules that make the world push back.

For each rule, state one consequence.

Clean water is scarce and contamination is not visible by taste.
Therefore, households depend on settling, tests, trusted warnings, and controlled storage.

3. Material life

Show how people eat, work, move, rest, store, repair, or prepare because of the constraints.

Do not describe an average citizen. Name variations created by location, work, or access.

4. Institutions and power

State:

  • the official rule
  • the real incentive
  • who can enforce it
  • who benefits
  • where resistance enters

5. Speculative capability

Describe one technology or magic through:

promise -> input -> output -> limit -> failure signal -> access -> social consequence

6. Culture and memory

Include one practice or symbol connected to repeated pressure. Show an official meaning and one competing meaning.

7. Causal map and timeline

Name only the locations, routes, durations, barriers, and event order needed to test the live conflict.

8. Pressure points

Write three choices that cost something. Each choice should connect at least three earlier sections.

9. Open questions

Leave two to four questions with bounded possibilities. A good open question has evidence, stakes, and more than one coherent answer.

Plain meaning:

A world brief is a decision tool, not a storage container.

In Nima:

It helps another creator reason about a warning, a failed stone, a public ritual, and a disputed record without learning every canal name.

Technical name:

This is a creator-facing specification: a compact statement of constraints, relationships, interfaces, and deliberate unknowns that someone else can use.

Walkthrough: Build the Nima Brief

Start from raw notes and compress them step by step.

Stage Input Transition Intermediate result Decision or output
1. Collect Notes about water, Authority law, stones, cups, routes, and records. Group facts by causal layer. Six connected systems become visible. Remove facts with no link to the council vote.
2. Constrain Scarcity, hidden contamination, unequal sunlight. Add one consequence per constraint. Daily routines and unequal safety appear. Keep only consequences that change action.
3. Expose power Authority control and marsh evidence. Trace permission from warning to sluice closure. The institutional bottleneck becomes visible. Center the brief on the warning-law choice.
4. Add meaning Empty cups, black reed, official ledger, work songs. Connect symbols to competing memories. Public ritual becomes part of current politics. Preserve disagreement instead of declaring one shared meaning.
5. Test causality Water takes seven hours; Mara's route takes four plus delay. Separate arrival, handling, decision, and recording. The late ledger no longer proves a late arrival. Keep the internal delay as an open question.
6. Audit Twelve canal names and three sunstone origin theories remain. Apply keep, imply, question, or cut. The active artifact becomes shorter. Deliver a brief that supports choices rather than a lore catalogue.

The naive path collects facts until the world looks full.

The capstone path starts with a decision, connects the systems that constrain it, and removes details that do not help another creator reason.

Proposed World Brief: Nima

World promise

In Nima, survival depends on moving water, warnings, and authority through unequal routes, while the city argues about who saved it from an earlier contamination crisis.

Constraints and material life

Clean water is scarce. Contamination is often invisible. Households settle muddy water, store it in sealed jars, and depend on tests or trusted warnings before drinking.

The upper district receives longer sunlight. Its roofs can charge more sunstones. Lower Gate receives less light and depends more heavily on public racks, cistern inspection, and shared reserves. A delayed warning therefore harms districts differently.

Institution and power

The Cistern Authority controls the main sluices, certifies sunstones, records warnings, and can close distribution. Its public promise is coordinated safety. Its incentive is to keep one chain of command and protect its legitimacy.

Marsh workers observe the intake first but cannot order a closure. Lower Gate families bear much of the risk. The proposed law would let marsh workers send a recognized emergency signal directly to the sluice.

Speculative capability

A charged sunstone sterilizes one sealed jar after several hours. It needs direct sunlight and water clear enough for light to pass through. One charge treats one jar. Cracked or worn stones may glow while treating only part of the water, so public test strips remain necessary.

Sunstones reduce disease risk. They do not create water, remove salt, guarantee fair distribution, or replace maintenance. Roof access and inspection make the capability unequal.

Culture and memory

During the dry-season ceremony, families display an empty cup tied with black reed. The Authority presents the cup as a symbol of disciplined rationing during the Year of Bitter Water.

Marsh families invert the cup and tie a broken knot. Their practice remembers workers whose warning was ignored. The same ceremony can express unity, accusation, grief, or refusal.

Map and timeline

Contaminated water needs about seven hours to reach Lower Gate from the North Marsh intake. A runner needs four hours by the raised road, plus any checkpoint delay. A closure order needs about twenty minutes from the Authority Hall to the sluice.

During the old crisis, route evidence supports Mara reaching the Hall at about 11:35. Distribution began at 13:00. The official ledger records the warning at 15:10. Arrival, handling, decision, and recording are not the same event.

Pressure points

  1. Mara can reveal the checkpoint tablet before the council vote. This may support direct warnings, but it also challenges the public memory that gives the Authority legitimacy.
  2. Lower Gate inspectors can announce today's cracked sunstone immediately. The announcement protects families but may cause a rush on limited reserves before replacement stones arrive.
  3. The Authority can accept a direct signal while keeping closure power. This speeds evidence but leaves the final bottleneck inside the Hall.

Open questions

  • Who handled Mara's warning between 11:35 and the 15:10 ledger entry?
  • Was the late record neglect, procedure, deliberate rewriting, or a second delivery?
  • Can a direct marsh signal be trusted without giving the Authority a new excuse to punish false alarms?
  • Will upper-district families support a law that weakens the system from which they benefit?

This brief gives another creator rules, consequences, conflicts, evidence, and unknowns. It does not decide how the council votes.

Check: Why does the Nima brief explain how a sunstone fails but not where sunstones ultimately come from?

Think first, then reveal.

Answer: Failure changes immediate decisions about water, testing, and trust. Ultimate origin can remain open because no current pressure depends on it. Add the origin only if it changes access, behavior, conflict, or interpretation.

Use Test: Can Someone Else Make a Choice?

Hand the brief to a reader or pretend you have forgotten your private notes. Ask for three predictions.

For example:

Heavy cloud covers Nima for five days. What changes?

A usable brief supports several grounded consequences:

  • fewer sunstones charge, especially in Lower Gate
  • public racks and stored reserves become more valuable
  • inspectors face pressure to approve worn stones
  • the Authority gains emergency power while critics demand direct warnings
  • the empty-cup ceremony may feel less historical and more immediate

The brief does not prescribe one plot. It constrains a family of plausible responses.

Check: A reader predicts that everyone simply creates unlimited clean water. What does that signal?

Think first, then reveal.

Answer: The brief has failed to communicate a non-negotiable constraint, or the reader has ignored it. Sunstones sterilize one existing jar under bounded conditions; they do not create water. Repair the brief by making that boundary visible near the capability.

Failure Review

Before approval, look for these failures.

Disconnected sections

The ecology, institution, capability, and culture appear as separate lists.

Repair: add arrows. Show how scarcity creates routines, how routines create control, and how control enters memory and conflict.

A cost-free capability

Technology or magic solves the pressure without changing infrastructure, work, access, or risk.

Repair: restore inputs, limits, failure signals, and unequal access.

A culture with one mind

Every person shares the same meaning and practice.

Repair: keep one shared pattern, then add variation grounded in position, work, generation, place, or interest.

A decorative map

Locations have names but no travel time, barrier, flow, permission, or consequence.

Repair: map only what changes movement, knowledge, or control.

Fake mystery

The brief hides a basic rule and calls the confusion intrigue.

Repair: make actionable rules clear. Leave questions open only where several coherent answers have evidence and stakes.

A closed world

Every conflict has a correct answer and every unknown has already been solved in private notes.

Repair: preserve questions that another creator can investigate or answer through a new artifact.

Trade-offs and Boundaries

A brief buys usability by giving up completeness.

  • More explicit rules improve consistency but can reduce room for discovery.
  • More open questions invite creation but can become arbitrary without bounded possibilities.
  • More examples improve clarity but can make one scenario look like the only valid use.
  • More detail preserves nuance but increases the cost of finding the active pressure.
  • Less detail improves speed but can hide the causal link needed for a fair decision.

The boundary signal is whether another creator can act.

If they repeatedly ask what is possible, add a rule. If they can predict only one approved outcome, reopen the world. If they ignore half the brief without changing a decision, cut inactive detail.

The central trade-off is structure versus openness. A good brief is firm at the level of consequences and generous at the level of possible responses.

Final Challenge: Build Your Brief

Choose the Glass Orchard or a world of your own.

If you use the Glass Orchard, begin with this seed:

Fruit becomes poisonous one hour after picking. Frost threads preserve sealed baskets for one day. Cooperatives control the living trees that recharge the threads. A market registry records an untreated basket arriving by an impossibly fast route.

Produce a brief with all nine sections:

  1. world promise
  2. non-negotiable constraints
  3. material life
  4. institutions and power
  5. speculative capability
  6. culture and memory
  7. causal map and timeline
  8. three pressure points
  9. two to four open questions

Then run two tests:

  • Change one constraint and predict at least three consequences.
  • Give the brief to another person, or simulate a cold read, and ask for one decision you did not write in advance.

Evidence and Readiness Rubric

Score each dimension from 0 to 2.

Dimension 0 1 2
Constraints Labels only Rules exist Rules produce visible consequences
Material life Missing One routine Several routines vary by access or place
Power Institution named Rules described Incentives, enforcement, benefit, and resistance connect
Capability Effect only Some limits Inputs, outputs, failures, access, and consequences align
Culture and memory Aesthetic markers Shared practice Practice connects pressure, memory, variation, and conflict
Map and timeline Decorative Routes or dates Movement, duration, knowledge, and control form a causal chain
Narrative pressure No choice Choice stated Several costly choices emerge from the systems
Selective detail Lore dump Some focus Every visible detail changes action or interpretation
Open questions Arbitrary gaps Questions listed Questions have bounded possibilities, evidence, and stakes
Usability Requires author explanation Supports one obvious use Another creator can predict, choose, and extend

A score of 16 or more suggests the brief is ready for another creator. The number is not a guarantee. Any zero in constraints, pressure, or usability is a stronger warning than the total score.

Revise the weakest causal link before adding more lore.

Key Takeaways

  • A speculative world brief is a decision tool for another creator, not a complete archive.
  • Connect every major layer through consequences: constraint, routine, power, capability, culture, route, and choice.
  • State rules clearly enough for fair prediction while preserving bounded questions for discovery.
  • Test the brief by changing one constraint and by asking someone else to make a grounded choice.
  • Revise weak causal links before adding more names, history, or lore.
