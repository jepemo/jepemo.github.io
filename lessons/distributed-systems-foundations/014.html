Foundations Review Check

Aha!

Consider a support ticket that says an order timed out, was retried, and then appeared twice in two dashboards.

This penultimate unit is the track review check. Unlike the initial check, it can now use both conceptual scenarios and specific vocabulary from the track.

Use it to test whether the concepts have become design judgment: latency is not only speed, a retry is not automatically safe, a replica is not automatically current, and a playbook is not useful unless it names evidence and action.

Why This Matters

This lesson matters because Distributed Systems Foundations is not just a sequence of distributed-systems terms. It is a way to reason about user-visible promises when state, time, messages, and failure are spread across independent components. The practical value is being able to name what the system can know, what it cannot know yet, and which promise should survive while uncertainty is resolved.

Core Walkthrough

Coverage Map

The check samples the full foundation layer:

  • network boundaries, latency, and partial failure
  • retries, idempotency, and fault handling
  • coordination, consensus, and quorums at a high level
  • clocks, causality, CAP, and PACELC
  • replication, partitioning, consistency, and gossip
  • observability, backpressure, contracts, degraded modes, and incident evidence

Answering Strategy

For each question, avoid answering from a slogan. Translate the situation into four parts: the promise, the local evidence, the missing evidence, and the mechanism that makes the trade-off explicit.

If two answers seem plausible, prefer the one that states what remains true during delay, duplicate delivery, overload, or recovery.

Failure Modes and Misconceptions

  • Misconception: Treating Foundations Review Check as a slogan instead of a design pressure.
  • Correction: Name the system promise, the available evidence, and the trade-off before choosing a mechanism.
  • Operational fix: Make the uncertain state visible in names, logs, tests, or review prompts.
